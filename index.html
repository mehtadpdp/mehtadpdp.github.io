Overview

A governance obligation, not a technology project

Getting from where you are today to a fully compliant programme realistically takes the better part of a year, sometimes longer, once you account for locating the data, fixing the gaps and embedding new habits across the business. Every month spent deciding to start is a month taken off that runway — and the law does not stay within India's borders either: any organisation abroad that serves customers here is caught by it too.

Because the Board is ultimately the one answerable for it, this sits with governance, not IT. It is the lens Mehta & Mehta has applied to every DPDP engagement since the Rules came into force.

Understand the Act & Penalties

Who Must Comply

  • Any Data Fiduciary — meaning any organisation that decides why and how digital personal data gets processed, whether it does so alone or jointly.
  • Any Data Processor working on a Fiduciary's behalf — Section 8(2) keeps liability with the Fiduciary no matter what the vendor contract promises.
  • Foreign entities too, wherever they are based, if they offer goods or services to people in India (Section 3).
  • Firms designated Significant Data Fiduciaries under Section 10 face a heavier compliance load under Rule 13.
